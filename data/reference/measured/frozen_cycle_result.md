# Frozen-detector cycle: result

Generated by `tools/build_frozen_cycle_result.py` from the CSVs in `data/`.
The preregistration is `docs/frozen_cycle_preregistration_2026-08-23.md` and is
unchanged. Every number below is a mean over seeds 0, 1, 2 at a matched oracle cost of 3600 annotated objects
across 4 rounds.

## The short version

**Selection works.** The best arm (`mult_prior_shrunk`) puts 601 tail-class objects in front of the annotator for the same 3600 boxes that `random` spends to reach 198 — a factor of 3.0.

**The gate does what it is for.** Turning the coherence factor on, one variable apart, takes isolated background from 1753 annotated proposals to 1371 — a 22% cut, in every seed.

**The plan's diagnosis half-holds.** `diversity_only` — pure diversity, the arm the plan's objection is aimed at — wastes 1938 proposals on isolated background, 2nd of 10 arms and 1.6x random's 1205. It was predicted to be the worst and is not quite: `mult_prior_shrunk` spends 1968, 2% more. Section 5 records that as a failed prediction.

**And it does not convert.** Every arm's `revealed_tail_map50` lands between 0.14 and 0.33. That is not a property of this surrogate: the real fine-tuned detector reaches tail AP50 of 0.001–0.909 on the same benchmark after up to 20000 annotations (`data/efficiency_curve.csv`). The block is downstream of selection, and section 4 measures where.

retention figure above is measured against it.

Read the `revealed_tail_map50` column as noise, not as a ranking. It puts `plan_full` second and `random` mid-table on a scale whose ceiling section 4 measures at under one AP50. The selection columns are where the signal is.

Two things in that table are worth saying out loud. **Equation 1 as written (250 tail objects) does not beat random (198).** It annotates 345 images for its 3600 boxes where the free prior annotates 525: the gated score prefers *crowded* images, because crowding is what local density measures, and crowded images are head-class images. The gate suppresses isolated junk and the same preference suppresses the tail with it.

**The corrected form does beat it.** `mult_prior_shrunk` — object-likeness as a multiplier instead of a summand, and `n̂_c` anchored on revealed labels with evidence-proportional shrinkage — reaches 601. So the plan's *shape* is what fails, not its idea.

## 2. Forgetting, and the exemplar allocation

| exemplar memory | t1 retention mAP50 | share of anchor | revealed_tail_map50 | revealed mAP50 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| none | 36.38 | 70% | 0.25 | 0.16 |
| α = -1 | 44.99 | 86% | 0.30 | 0.21 |
| α = -0.5 | 46.72 | 90% | 0.34 | 0.23 |
| α = 0 | 44.89 | 86% | 0.49 | 0.29 |
| α = 0.5 | 45.79 | 88% | 0.38 | 0.28 |
| α = 1 | 44.50 | 85% | 0.27 | 0.18 |

Fine-tuning on each round's annotations alone costs 30% of the head's task-1 mAP50 (52.07 → 36.38). The real detector loses 98% on the same benchmark (73.65 → 1.69, `data/efficiency_curve.csv`), so the surrogate at its default strength **understates** forgetting — and therefore understates what a memory is worth. That direction is deliberate; the next table shows the whole range.

Replay restores it, at every α tried: from 36.38 up to 44.50–46.72, best at α = -0.5 (90% of the anchor). The memory is the same size at every α; only its composition changes.

**The predicted trade-off is absent.** The preregistration expected replay to buy stability at the cost of plasticity, and said in advance that no cost would mean the measurement is too weak to be believed. There is no cost: at α = −1 retention goes 36.38 → 44.99 and tail AP goes 0.25 → 0.30. Read the second number with section 4 in mind: on an endpoint whose ceiling is under one AP50, a change of a tenth is noise. The measurement is indeed too weak on that axis, exactly as the preregistration allowed for.

### How much forgetting there is to fix is not a property of the method

The table above sits at one fine-tuning strength, and that strength is a dial. Setting it to flatter a method would be the easiest mistake available here, so the whole range is reported. Each cell is the share of the anchor's task-1 mAP50 that survives 4 rounds, averaged over the same seeds.

| fine-tuning strength | no replay | α = -1 | α = -0.5 | α = 0 | α = 0.5 | α = 1 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.1 | 101% | 97% | 98% | 97% | 97% | 97% |
| 0.3 | 71% | 88% | 91% | 88% | 89% | 87% |
| 0.6 | 18% | 42% | 68% | 41% | 83% | 25% |
| 1 | 2% | 9% | 10% | 1% | 8% | 1% |

Three things follow, and only these three.

1. **Replay is only worth anything when there is something to fix.** At strength 0.1 the head keeps 101% of its task-1 mAP50 with no memory at all, and every α does slightly worse than that. A method evaluated only there would look useless; a method evaluated only at one strength would look like whatever that strength made it look like.

2. **Where there is forgetting, the memory recovers a large part of it.** At strength 0.3 it adds +20 points, strength 0.6 it adds +65 points, strength 1 it adds +7 points. The largest single recovery in the table is +65 points.

3. **Which α is best is not resolved, and the plan's prediction is neither confirmed nor refuted.** The plan reasons that rare classes forget fastest, so the memory should favour them: α < 0. Grouping the columns that way:

| strength | α < 0, mean retained | α ≥ 0, mean retained | spread across α |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.3 | 89% | 88% | 4 points |
| 0.6 | 55% | 49% | 59 points |
| 1 | 9% | 4% | 9 points |

   The tail-favouring group is ahead in 3 of 3 rows, but in every row the spread between individual α values is larger than the gap between the two groups. Three seeds cannot separate them. What survives is the direction, not a number — and note that the predecessor repository, measuring image-level storage by a different route, found the exponent *reversed* (`docs/round_selection_preregistration.md`, and the α = −1 request that realised as +0.54). Two instantiations, two answers, neither decisive: this is the open question of contribution B, and it needs seeds, not prose.

One row is a warning rather than a result. At strength 1 the head keeps 2% without replay and at most 10% with it. That strength is what it takes to reproduce the real detector's 98% loss, and there a 128-unit head has nothing left to replay into. It is a limit of the surrogate, not a finding about memory, and it is why the default sits at the moderate strength instead.

**This is the answer to the question the plan left open, and it is not the value in the code.** The committed coefficients are γ = 0.5, λ = 0.2, fixed before any of this was measured and not changed by it. On tail discovery that cell reaches 250 objects and ranks 14 of 20. The best cell is γ = 2.0, λ = 0.1 at 359, 1.4× the committed one, and it wastes 1089 isolated proposals against the committed cell's 1371 — so it is better on both axes at once. The worst is γ = 0.5, λ = 0.0 at 135.

Read that with the shape of the surface in mind. Down the λ = 0.0 column the tail count goes 160 → 202 → 135 → 300 → 351, which is **not monotone** in γ. A surface this rough at three seeds is a reason to re-run the sweep with more of them before writing a number into the code, not a reason to write this one in. The arms in section 1 are deliberately not re-run at swept values: doing that would turn every contrast there into a comparison between two tuned scores.

The waste surface is the cleaner of the two, and it says what the gate is for: holding λ at 0.4 and raising γ from 0.0 to 2.0 takes isolated background from 1556 to 1037. The coherence factor does suppress isolated junk, monotonically, exactly as the plan argues it should.

## 4. Why the detection endpoints are flat

Two limits sit above every AP in section 1, and both belong to the exported checkpoint rather than to any selection rule. A proposal set cannot detect an object it does not overlap, and a reachable object still has to be separable. `owodtail/ceiling.py` measures both — the second by fitting a linear probe per class on **every label in the candidate pool**, a budget no campaign would have.

| classes | band | reachable objects | probe AP50 with all labels |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| known at export (t1) | head | 75.1% | 34.55 |
| known at export (t1) | medium | 95.3% | 48.80 |
| unknown at export | head | 11.6% | 0.16 |
| unknown at export | medium | 12.5% | 0.06 |
| unknown at export | tail | 23.1% | 0.23 |

That is the result. On the classes it was trained on, PROB's frozen decoder feature is a good representation — three quarters of the objects are reachable and a linear probe reaches tens of AP50. On the classes it was not trained on, an eighth to a quarter of the objects are reachable and a linear probe reaches under one AP50 *with every label available*. The annotation-to-detection pathway is blocked by the representation, two orders of magnitude before the acquisition score gets a say.

## 5. The predictions, checked

| prediction | measured | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 1. `diversity_only` wastes the most budget on isolated background | worst arm is `mult_prior_shrunk` at 1968 | **not supported** |
| 2. the gate lowers isolated waste | 1753 → 1371, and in every seed | supported |
| 3. every scored arm beats random on tail discovery | 2 do not: `plan_uncertainty`, `plan_full` | **not supported** |
| 4. the free prior beats equation 1 on tail discovery | 538 vs 250 | supported |
| 5. the distribution term adds on top of the prior | `prior_full` 425 vs `objectness_prior` 538 | **not supported** |
| 6. fine-tuning without replay costs at least half of t1 mAP50 | 52.07 → 36.38, a 30% loss | **not supported** |
| 7. replay at α = −1 restores t1 mAP50 to within 20%, and costs tail AP | restored to 86% (holds); tail AP 0.25 → 0.30 (does not cost) | **not supported** |

2 of 7 supported; 1, 3, 5, 6, 7 did not. None is removed and none is softened, and the failures are not all the same kind of failure:

* **Wrong about the world.** Prediction 5: the distribution term does not add to the free prior in the additive form the plan writes. Prediction 3: two scored arms, including the plan's own equation, lose to random on the endpoint the plan cares about most.
* **Wrong about the ranking, not the mechanism.** Prediction 1: `diversity_only` is a heavy waster of budget, as predicted, but not the heaviest. The gate contrast in prediction 2 — the same score with and without the coherence factor — is the clean test of the mechanism, and it holds in every seed.
* **Wrong about the surrogate, by design.** Predictions 6 and 7 are about how much the head forgets and what replay costs. Section 2 shows both depend on a fine-tuning strength that is a free dial, deliberately left where it understates forgetting. A prediction stated at one point on a dial was a badly posed prediction, and that is this document's fault rather than the plan's.

## 7. What this cannot say

**Five of the ten arms do not reproduce across scikit-learn builds.** The pseudo-class estimate is `MiniBatchKMeans`, which is deterministic given a seed inside one build and not between builds, so the five arms that need it — `plan_full`, `plan_full_no_gate`, `plan_radius_core`, `plan_full_forgetting`, `prior_full` — land within a few percent of the numbers above rather than on them. Re-running this grid on scikit-learn 1.7 moved them by −5% to +9% on tail objects and changed no ordering and no conclusion. The other five arms are bit-exact, which is what isolates the cause. The committed numbers come from numpy 2.5.2 and scikit-learn 1.9.0; replacing the clustering with a deterministic implementation is the fix and is not done.

**One prediction's verdict is not stable, and that is the point.** Prediction 7 turns on a tail-AP difference of a tenth of a point, on an endpoint whose ceiling section 4 measures at under one AP50. It reads as supported on one build and as failed on another. Nothing about the pipeline changed between them. An endpoint with no headroom cannot decide anything, which is why the selection endpoints carry this study.

The backbone is frozen, so boxes never improve and unknown coverage is capped at what `t1.pth` proposes. The learner is a 128-unit head, not a detector: it makes the direction of an effect visible on a laptop and says nothing reliable about magnitude, except where it was checked against the real run and agreed. Three seeds support a sign test and not a p-value. Every baseline here was run inside this framework on this pool; none is quoted from another paper.
