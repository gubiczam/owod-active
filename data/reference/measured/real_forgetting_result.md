# Real-detector forgetting, by acquisition method

> **Superseded in part.** This reports the run that had `random` at 600 regions only. The grid is now complete at 600 / 3 500 / 20 000 for all three arms, and the forgetting it measures has since been traced to a cause and largely removed. The current results are `docs/real_group_forgetting_result.md` and `docs/research_plan_coverage.md`. What is still accurate here: the b600 numbers, the preregistration deviation, and the proxy-vs-real ranking reversal. What is stale: the closing claim that a curve would need two more GPU-hours — it was run, and `random` forgets most at every budget.

Generated by `tools/build_real_forgetting_result.py`. Every number comes from `data/efficiency_curve_random_b600.csv`, which the GPU run wrote, or from `data/frozen_cycle_arms.csv`.

## What was run

**The detector's weights are updated.** PROB's own bridge is called as `train --previous-checkpoint t1.pth --output-checkpoint <new>`; it writes a new 478 MB checkpoint and the official evaluator scores that checkpoint. This is not the frozen-feature surrogate.

| | |
|---|---|
| pipeline | `distribution-aware-owod/notebooks/efficiency_curve.ipynb`, branch `feat/contribution-a` |
| transition | t1 → t2, `PREV=19`, `CUR=21` |
| starting weights | `exps/SOWODB/PROB/t1.pth`, identical for every arm |
| oracle cost | **600 annotated regions per arm**, identical |
| epochs / LR / batch | 1 / 2e-5 / 1 — the bridge defaults, not tuned |
| evaluator | the official PROB evaluator, `--batch-size 4`, separate pass |
| exemplar memory | off |
| seeds | **one** (seed 0) |
| anchor | previous-19 mAP50 = **73.649** before any t2 training |

## Disclosure: `random` was not in the preregistered grid

`distribution-aware-owod/docs/efficiency_curve_preregistration.md` declares **two** arms and justifies each: `objectness_prior` as the free control and `mult_prior_shrunk` as the best distribution-aware arm. `random` is not among them.

It was added afterwards, at the supervisor's request, and this is recorded as a deviation rather than folded in silently. What that costs: the arm was chosen after the other two had been measured, so it cannot inherit the preregistration's protection. What it does not cost: the *grid* is unchanged for the other two arms — their b600 points were already complete and were skipped, so their numbers here are the previously published ones, not a re-run.

The budget was also narrowed from three points to one (600 only), so the run cost one training point instead of six. The three-point curve in `data/efficiency_curve.csv` is untouched; this run writes a separate file.

## The result

| arm | oracle cost | unknowns revealed | kept images | t2 objects trained on | previous-19 final | forgetting | tail AP50 | U-Recall |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `random` | 600 | 5 | 232 of 591 | 690 (1.15×) | 27.27 | −46.38 (63.0%) | 0.0000 | 37.09 |
| `mult_prior_shrunk` | 600 | 53 | 189 of 519 | 417 (0.69×) | 46.64 | −27.01 (36.7%) | 0.0010 | 27.21 |
| `objectness_prior` | 600 | 84 | 255 of 585 | 535 (0.89×) | 59.19 | −14.46 (19.6%) | 0.9091 | 22.02 |

**The answer to the question that was asked.** At the same 600-region cost, the distribution-aware arm keeps 46.64 previous-19 mAP50 where random keeps 27.27 — **19.37 points less forgetting**. On the real detector, with the weights actually updated.

**And the free control beats both.** `objectness_prior` keeps 59.19, forgetting only 14.46. It also produced the only non-negligible tail AP50 in the run (0.9091) and revealed the most unknown objects (84). The pattern the earlier discovery work found — that nothing beats the free objectness prior — holds here too, now on forgetting as well as on discovery.

**The tail endpoint is empty.** Two of three arms score under 0.002 tail AP50, and the preregistered targets are T50 = 21.18 and T75 = 31.77. No arm is within two orders of magnitude. This is the same wall `docs/frozen_cycle_result.md` §4 measures as a representation ceiling, and one seed of real fine-tuning does not move it.

## The frozen-feature surrogate orders these arms the other way round

| arm | real detector, final prev-19 | frozen-feature proxy, final task-1 |
|---|---:|---:|
| `random` | 27.27 | 39.29 |
| `mult_prior_shrunk` | 46.64 | 29.49 |
| `objectness_prior` | 59.19 | 22.67 |
| *anchor* | *73.65* | *51.27* |

Real detector, best retention first: `objectness_prior` > `mult_prior_shrunk` > `random`.
Proxy, best retention first: `random` > `mult_prior_shrunk` > `objectness_prior`.

**The orderings are exactly reversed.** The arm the proxy says forgets least is the arm the real detector says forgets most.

This is a correction to a claim made earlier in this repository. The surrogate was checked against the real run on *magnitude* — both land in the catastrophic regime, both put tail AP50 under one point — and it passed. It was never checked on *ranking between acquisition methods*, and it fails that. So: the frozen pool is fine for measuring what a score selects, and it must not be used to rank acquisition methods on forgetting. That comparison needs the detector.

## What is held equal, and what is not

| quantity | equal? | why |
|---|---|---|
| annotated regions | **yes, 600 each** | by construction: `campaign.run` selects proposals, and the oracle is paid per region it is asked about |
| starting weights, epochs, LR, batch, evaluator, memory | **yes** | one parameter block, one bridge invocation shape |
| selected images | no | 591, 519, 585 — a consequence of how each arm concentrates its regions |
| kept training images | no | 232, 189, 255 — PROB's t2 loader keeps only the 21 t2 classes, so an image whose unknowns are all t3/t4 arrives with zero boxes and is dropped |
| gradient steps | no | equal to kept images, since epochs = 1 and batch = 1 |
| t2 objects trained on | no | 690, 417, 535 — PROB trains on *every* t2 box in a selected image, not only the queried regions |

The last row is the subtlety worth stating out loud. The oracle cost is equal, but the *supervision* the training actually receives is not, and it is not proportional to what the acquisition discovered: random revealed 5 true unknown objects and still trained on 690 t2 boxes, because PROB uses the full annotation of every image it is given.

**No mechanism is established.** Of the candidate explanations, `U-Recall` order the same way as forgetting across the three arms, and the rest do not. With three arms a monotone ordering arises by chance one time in three, so this is not evidence. Attributing the difference to supervision volume would be unsupported.

## What this cannot say

* **No significance.** One seed, one budget point, one realisation per arm. No spread, no p-value. The preregistration says this in advance for the two original arms and it applies to the third.
* **Not a curve.** One budget point cannot support "reaches the same tail level from fewer annotations". That needs the 3 500 and 20 000 points for `random`, which is roughly two more GPU-hours.
* **Not a mechanism**, per the section above.
* **Not a tail result.** Two arms score under 0.002 and the third under 1.0, against a target of 21.18.
* **Not "our method reduces forgetting"** as a property. It forgets less than random at this one point; the free control forgets less than both.
